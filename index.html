PrintCaddy - Free 3D CAD for 3D printing in your browser

Welcome to PrintCaddy

PrintCaddy is CAD for 3D printing, right in your browser - as easy to use as Tinkercad, but a step towards Fusion 360. Free, no account, no installation - and what you build stays on this computer (or, if you host it yourself, on your own server).

Know Tinkercad? Then you will find your way at once: drag shapes onto the workplane, group holes, align, mirror, bundle, Alt-drag for a copy - the controls are deliberately the same. You just no longer hit the limits that bothered you there. And under Settings → Appearance one click makes the work area look like it too. Your Tinkercad designs can come along too: a free tool from the community, not part of PrintCaddy and not yet tested by us, converts them into PrintCaddy files. Export to PrintCaddy

What PrintCaddy adds

  • Chamfer and fillet edges, a fillet with a radius that changes along the edge too, taken back or changed at any time, on exact CAD geometry all the way into the STEP file.
  • Hollow out bodies (even wall thickness) and split them with a plane, slanted too.
  • Sketches with exact measurements and rounded corners that you extrude into bodies, revolve around an axis or pull along a path.
  • Threads: screws, nuts, threaded rods and tapped holes, metric, UNC/UNF and Whitworth.
  • Patterns in a row, circle or spiral, and wrapping shapes around a cylinder.
  • STEP exchange with CAD programs, plus STL, 3MF, OBJ, SVG and DXF - plus measuring, a section view and a history to look back at earlier states.
  • Printer profiles for around 220 printers with an overhang warning, marked overhangs and a filament estimate.
  • An AI can build along on request, through the MCP interface - with a copy of PrintCaddy that you run yourself.
  1. Start a design“Create a new 3D design” opens an empty workplane. An existing file goes through the tile next to it.
  2. Place shapesDrag a box, cylinder, text or any other shape from the shape library (Shapes, top left in the ribbon) onto the workplane, then set size and position. Sizes take percentages too: 50% halves them. "Transparent" lets you look through a body.
  3. Group in the cut-outsEvery shape is either a solid or a cut-out. Grouping makes the cut-out remove material - that is how holes, slots and pockets are made. The object list (Ctrl+Shift+O) shows every part, grouped ones too, and whether it is a solid or a cut-out; its search finds parts inside groups as well. "Edit group" (E, or a double click) lets you change single parts of a group; "Done" rebuilds it.
  4. Break the edges and exportChamfer and fillet work on the edges you click. When done, send it to the slicer as STL or 3MF, or to CAD as STEP.

What else PrintCaddy does

  • Hollow: bodies with an even wall, open on any sides you choose (top, bottom, front, back, left, right, also combined) or fully closed - inner edges rounded or sharp.
  • Delete face: click the faces of a fillet, a hole or a boss and delete them - the faces around them grow to close the gap, and the body stays exact for more fillets and the STEP export.
  • Split: cut a body or a hole in two with a plane you move and tilt for an angled cut - handy for parts too big for the print bed.
  • Hull: wraps the selected shapes in one convex body, as if shrink-wrapped: a half sphere on a cylinder becomes a dome with no step, four corner posts a box with round corners. Holes in the selection are cut out of the hull afterwards, and Edit group opens it again.
  • Cookie cutter: makes a cookie cutter from a sketch, an SVG or any shape in one click: a thin wall round the outline and a flat rim round its foot. The outline is mirrored, so text comes out of the dough the right way round, and the wall runs up to a cutting edge. The inside of an e or an a gets a wall of its own, held by bridges, and wall, edge, height and rim can be changed afterwards under Properties.
  • Flexi joints: cuts a flat body - a fish, a snake or a dragon you drew yourself - into links with a joint printed in place at every cut, so the figure moves once printed, without support or assembly.
  • Loft sketches: joins two sketches or imported SVGs into one body: the outline of the lower one at the bottom, the outline of the upper one at the top, with straight walls between them - a funnel, a transition piece or, from two rings, a tapered frame. Edit group opens the two sketches again.
  • Sketches: draw outlines, lay a true arc through three points, curve any straight side (a double click on the body opens its sketch again), type a line's length and angle while drawing ("50<30") or snap it in 15° steps, type lines to an exact length and corners to an exact angle, fillet or chamfer corners, mirror a selection across an axis or a line, draw construction lines that points snap to, close a gap by dragging one open end onto the other, and extrude them or revolve them around an axis - or build just the line with a width, as a frame or a stripe.
  • Shape library: threads (metric, UNC/UNF and G pipe threads), bolts and nuts, gears, ring gears and racks with involute or round teeth that mesh, springs, bent pipes, lofts from one outline to another, twisted or tilted too, capsules with one smaller end, paraboloids, text (with the spacing between the letters set, including curved along an arc or stacked top to bottom, in fonts of your own, Arabic and Hebrew right to left too, as an outline, a silhouette or in layers as a name tag for a multicolour print, with a key ring hole - on a side or anywhere you put it - and rounded letter edges if you like; an SVG icon as a layered icon tag too), QR codes from a text or a web address (alone, raised on a plate or engraved), print-in-place hinges, Gridfinity bins (with compartments if you like) and baseplates, knurled grips (straight, crossed or round), dovetail joints, teardrop holes for horizontal bores, counterbores, countersinks and double countersinks for screw heads and more - all adjustable afterwards. Bodies you need again and again are kept as custom shapes - in the browser or on your own server - and go into any design with one click.
  • Patterns: repeat a shape n times in a row or around a circle, for hole grids or toothed rings - and scale several parts at once by a percentage. Alt on an align dot stretches parts to the side or the size of another instead of moving them.
  • Hole pattern: Click a flat face of a body and spread holes over it: round, square, rectangular, hexagonal or slots, in a grid or staggered, with spacing, margin to the edge and depth (through the whole body or a set depth). A preview shows the holes before you create them; they stay ordinary holes, grouped with the body - for vents, speaker grilles, baskets and enclosures.
  • Offset and cut out: grow or shrink an exact body by a set distance, for the clearance of a press fit, or cut one part out of another with a gap all round, in one step.
  • Surface texture: lay a relief onto any body, for grip or for looks - knurling, grooves, honeycomb, bricks, carbon fibre, tree bark, wood grain, rope, a picture as a height map or text, around round bodies or flat along an axis.
  • Vases and pots: a shape of its own for vases, pots and lampshades: body form, cross-section, twist, patterns, drain holes, a saucer and a lid - solid for the slicer's vase mode or hollow.
  • Bundle: hold parts together with Ctrl+B as in Tinkercad, without merging them - colours stay and every part is its own body in the export, handy for multicolour prints. A group shows every part in its own colour too, with the Multicolor switch, even after it was merged with holes.
  • Workplane on any face: place shapes straight onto slanted or side faces, and hide the plane for a clear view without dropping it.
  • Place by click: a new shape follows the pointer and lands where you click, near the middle of a face right in its middle - can be switched off in the settings.
  • Snap to other shapes: while moving, edges and centres lock onto neighbouring shapes and a guide line shows where - Alt during the drag pauses it. Shift keeps the move on one axis, and Alt held from the start drags a copy, as in Tinkercad. New shapes lie down on the face under the pointer, sloped ones too, and C sets the selection down on another face the same way, like Cruise in Tinkercad.
  • Snap grids of your own: add a measure such as the 2.54 mm pin pitch of chips and perfboard in the settings; the snap menu offers it whole, halved and quartered.
  • A clear view: the object list, the settings and the tool panels can be dragged anywhere on the workplane, dragging the view cube turns the view, a click on its corners and edges shows the model at an angle, and the plate can be hidden for a look from below. A right click on a body brings up the most used commands, and the view can be saved as a picture. A start view of your own opens new designs from the angle you like. A second view opens in a window of its own, with its own camera, and follows every change.
  • Rotation pivot: turn around the axis of a pipe end or the centre of a face instead of the body's centre. On a single body the pivot stays with it.
  • Lay flat on face: click a face and the part rests on the plate with that side down - the best print orientation in one click. "Align faces" does the same between two parts: click a face of one, then a face of the other, and they touch or sit flush, with an optional gap. "Same axis" puts a hole over a hole or a pin into a hole.
  • Measure: tape measure (a typed length moves the body to exactly that distance; its measurements stay with the design and can be hidden), ruler and framing square, distances to the origin - for several selected bodies too -, notes right on the workplane, reference images to line parts up on (a photo of a board under its case, calibrated to size), and a section view that cuts the model open to look inside, measures wall thickness and gaps on the cut and saves it as an SVG for a laser or a template.
  • Files with the design: notes and lists as .md or .txt, a photo of the printed part (.png, .jpg, .webp) or a datasheet as PDF stay inside the design and travel with the .lyl file. The eye shows a text or a picture in a window; a PDF is downloaded.
  • Save into your folder: in Chrome and Edge you choose the folder and name yourself, and Ctrl+S saves the design into that file again - a file you opened is replaced in place. Other browsers download it.
  • Back up all designs: one click packs every design into a single file; opening it brings them all back, in this browser or another. Old designs can be chosen together with Select on the start page and deleted in one go.
  • Ready to print: around 220 printers with their build plate; PrintCaddy warns when something reaches past the plate or is too tall, marks overhangs that would need supports and estimates weight and filament on export.
  • Import: STL, OBJ, 3MF, SVG and DXF (a part outline from a laser cutting shop comes in with its holes as holes of their own) with their colours, STEP, plus images as a reference - and parts from other PrintCaddy designs straight into the open one. An SVG comes in as a sketch: an area, a stroke outside or inside, or a silhouette without its holes, say for a cookie cutter, and switched to a revolve sketch it turns into a round body such as a vase. An SVG pattern or lettering can be wrapped around a cylinder, raised or engraved.
  • Your language: the program and its guide speak English, German and 17 more languages; the language list is at the very top right, and in the settings on the start page.
  • Works offline: after the first visit PrintCaddy also starts without internet - install it like a normal program, your designs stay on your computer.
  • Build with AI: through the MCP interface an AI assistant can create and edit shapes, in an editor it opens itself. That needs a copy you run yourself (npm run dev, or the Docker image); on the public site the AI connection is off.
  • Reference points: right-click a body to mark its centre, corners or edge middles with points that nothing prints and that shapes snap to - then place a hole exactly on the centre of another part.
  • Command search: Press Ctrl+K and type the name of a tool, a shape or a command to jump straight to it - in English or German, with the keyboard shortcuts shown beside each one. The bodies of your design are found by name as well.
  • Simplify meshes: An imported mesh with a very large number of triangles is reduced to a fraction, with a before-and-after view and a slider - so cutting, grouping and dragging stay smooth.
  • History: look back at any earlier state of the project with a slider, without changing anything - export that state, start a new project from it or bring single objects back from it.
  • Drawing for the workshop: Export → Drawing: front, top and side views at 1:1 on an ISO sheet, with overall sizes, hidden edges and a table of the round bores – as PDF or SVG, to print or to take to the lathe and mill. The top view as SVG or DXF goes to the laser cutter, with stencil bridges that hold the middle of an o or a b if you like.
  • Look as you like it: Background and workplane, axis arrows for X, Y and Z, edge lines on every body, the sketch colours, and the contrast, the shadows and the direction of the light are yours to set in the settings - for example so dark bodies do not run into each other. On slower computers, fast mode turns everything costly off at once.

Install as an app or host it yourself

Installed as an app, PrintCaddy starts like a normal program, with its own icon and window, even without internet - one click in Chrome or Edge is enough. If you would rather host it yourself so nothing leaves the house: on Windows one line in PowerShell is enough, and it adds a shortcut on the desktop. For a NAS or home server there is a ready-made Docker image for amd64 and arm64; there the designs can also live on the server, so every device in the house sees the same ones. The steps are in the guide chapter on installing and hosting it yourself.

In the editor, the Help group holds a short guide and the list of every keyboard shortcut. In the footer, "Bug report" saves your design with everything needed for a report in the forum or on GitHub.
