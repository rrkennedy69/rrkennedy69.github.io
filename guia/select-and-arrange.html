Selecting and arranging

Selecting shapes, finding them in the object list, aligning, mirroring, rotating, laying flat on a face and multiplying in patterns.

Selecting

A click on a shape selects it. With Shift you add more or take them away again. While Shift is held and two or more shapes are selected, a small number below the mouse pointer counts them. A click on empty space clears the selection, and so does Esc. Drag a frame on the empty workplane to select everything it touches; with Shift held, the frame adds what was not selected and takes away what was. If you would rather pick one part out of a cluster, switch on “A direção da moldura define a seleção” in the settings (area “Aparência”): a frame dragged to the right then takes only what lies completely inside it, one dragged to the left still everything it touches, and its line is dashed. Hold Alt while you drag to turn the frame around for that one drag, for example to take whole bodies from the side where the direction would not allow it. And Ctrl+A selects all visible bodies. While you drag a body or a handle of the frame, Esc cancels as long as the mouse button is still held: everything goes back as it was, and nothing is added to the history.

A shape behind another one, such as a hole inside a body, you reach without turning the view: right-click the spot and choose “Selecionar outro…”. The menu then lists every shape under the pointer, from front to back. The one your pointer is on is outlined in the view, even through the shapes in front of it; a click selects it, and with Shift it is added to the selection. The entry is there whenever more than one shape lies under the pointer; on a tablet, a long press opens the same menu.

What is in your way you hide (“Ocultar selecionados”, Ctrl+H). Hidden shapes stay in the design, they are just not visible and stay out of the export; the export window says beforehand how many there are. Ctrl+Shift+H brings them all back. What should stay put you lock with the padlock at the top of the settings or with Ctrl+L. A locked shape cannot be moved, resized, turned, recoloured or deleted: its handles turn grey, a padlock shows beside the arrow on top, and the tools that would change it are greyed out. Hovering a handle still shows its sizes, and a key that would change it says "Object locked". While one locked shape is in the selection, nothing changes for the others either. Hiding, renaming, copying and duplicating still work.

A number on the button of the group “Visibilidade” says how many shapes are hidden, and a click on it shows them all again. A design that is opened with hidden shapes says so once, with a button to show them.

The object list

With many parts it is easy to lose track in the view. The object list (“Mostrar lista de objetos” or Ctrl+Shift+O) shows all shapes as a list. It stays open or closed as you left it, for every design, until you close it; the choice is kept in the browser.

  • A click on an entry selects the shape, including parts inside a group. With Shift held you add more entries or take them away again, just as in the view. The arrows fold groups open and shut.
  • The label shows whether a part is “Sólido” or “Orifício”.
  • Padlock and eye lock and hide single parts.
  • “Renomear” gives a part a name of its own; the pencil beside the name at the top of the settings does the same for the selected shape. Sensible names help enormously in larger designs.
  • The search field finds shapes by name and by kind, so also "cylinder" or "hole". It also looks inside groups, however deeply nested: a group holding a match stays in the list and folds open to show the matching parts and the groups that lead to them. A group found by its own name shows all its parts when you open it.
  • The arrow on the far left of the title bar folds the list down to its title bar and back.

The list sits docked just right of the view cube. Drag it by its title bar to put it anywhere on the workplane; it passes underneath the view cube and the camera bar. Drop it back beside the view cube, or double-click the title bar, and it docks again. Where it was and whether it was folded are remembered in this browser.

Typing a position

The card “Posição” in the properties panel shows where the selected body stands, and you can type it: “X” and “Y” are the middle of its box on the plate, with the same signs as the distances shown on the workplane, and “Z (altura)” is the height of its underside. With “Measured from” at the top of the card you switch to “Edge”: X and Y then show the body's left and front edges, so you put an edge exactly on a measure. The setting stays until you switch back to “Centre”. X counts to the right, Y towards the back and Z up, by the right-hand rule as in Tinkercad and CAD programs; the axis arrows on the plate show the directions. The values go in the unit you set, and the sliders reach as far as dragging does.

Parking parts beside the plate

You can drag a shape past the edge of the plate and leave it there, about one plate width beyond each side, to keep it for later. The arrow keys move it as far as you like. A parked part still belongs to the design and goes into the export with everything else. To leave it out, hide it or export only the selection. If you have picked a printer, PrintCAD warns at export when something sits outside its plate.

Snapping while moving

When you drag a shape across the workplane, it snaps to other shapes: its left, right, front or back edge or its centre lands exactly on an edge or centre of another shape. A pink guide line shows what it is holding on to. That way two parts sit flush side by side or centred one behind the other without typing numbers. It works with the grid switched off, too.

Hold Shift while dragging to keep the move on one axis: the shape follows whichever direction, X or Y, you have dragged further in, and leaves the other alone. Best press Shift once you are already dragging; on a shape that is not selected yet, a click with Shift only adds it to the selection.

With Alt already held when the drag begins, you drag a copy, as in Tinkercad: the shape stays where it is and the copy lands where you let go. Pressing Alt only on the way pauses snapping; the shape then follows the grid only. A copy, too, snaps only once you let go of Alt on the way. To switch snapping off for good, use the grid menu in the bottom right (“Encaixar em formas”) or the settings. For now only moving on the normal workplane snaps, not lifting and not resizing.

Aligning

Two or more selected shapes are lined up with “Alinhar”: points appear around the selection for left, centre and right, front, centre and back, and top, centre and bottom. A click on a point moves all shapes there. If you first click one of the selected shapes, it stays in place and the others line up with it. A locked shape in the selection is that fixed shape by itself. Left, right, front, back, top and bottom go by the box round a shape; the centre of a triangle, a pentagon or a star is its own middle, so a hole centred on one sits in the middle of the shape and not of its box.

A shape turned at an angle, say by 45°, has its box round it to line up with, not its own edges. To line up with the edges, set a workplane on a face of that shape first (“Posicionar plano de trabalho”, then click the face): the points then stand on the axes of that plane, and the shapes are measured and moved along them, as in Tinkercad. Reset the workplane to line up along the plate again.

The key is L. Esc cancels.

Matching sizes

Hold Alt while aligning and the dots turn into squares: a click no longer moves the shapes but changes their size. A dot at a side stretches each shape to that side, its other side staying where it is, as if you had dragged its handle there. The dot in the middle leaves each shape in its place and gives it the width, length or height in that direction. That way you swap a box for a cylinder of the same size, say, or replace a broken piece of a mesh with a clean shape. Move over a dot with Alt held and you see beforehand how large the shapes become.

The reference is the shape you clicked first; only the others change. Without one, the side dots stretch all shapes to the farthest side, and the middle dot gives them all the size of the largest. A cylinder stays round, so it grows in the other direction too. A shape turned at an angle stays as it is; shapes turned by 90° work.

Mirroring

“Espelhar” (M, or right-click the selection and choose it) flips the selection across a plane: “esquerda-direita”, “frente-trás” or “cima-baixo”. Click the matching axis arrow. That is handy for symmetric parts: build one half, copy it and mirror the copy.

Rotating and setting the pivot

You rotate with the curved arrows on the shape, with the numbers in the settings or with R in 45° steps. When you drag on the rotation circle, the turn snaps to every tick, that is every 22.5°; hold Shift for finer steps, and extra ticks appear every 11.25°. Drag outside the circle to turn freely in 1° steps, or in 45° steps with Shift. Normally the selection turns about its centre. Sometimes it should not, for example when a tilted tube is to be turned further at its end. For that there is “Definir ponto de rotação”: afterwards click on a face. A flat face supplies its centre, for example the axis of a tube end. On a group or a bundle the face runs on across parts that meet, as on one body, and the pivot goes to the middle of the whole face once the parts are joined, except on a group or bundle with text in it, or a bundle with a hole among its parts. The selection now turns about that point. A second click on the tool removes it again.

The angles stay with the body. In its settings, “Rotação” shows them as “Em torno de X”, “Em torno de Y” and “Em torno de Z (altura)”, named like the position: Y runs across the plate, Z is up. Type an angle there and the body turns to it - about its centre, or about its own pivot if it has one. While a turned body is selected, its angles also show below it on the workplane; “Mostrar os ângulos de sólidos girados” in the settings switches that off.

With one body selected, the pivot belongs to that body: select other parts and come back, and it is still there. It moves, turns and scales with the body, is saved in the project and comes back with Undo. Under “Posição” the pivot has its own X, Y and Z, which you can type or nudge, and “Remover” takes it away. You can also drag the pivot's red marker with the mouse: it slides over the surface of the bodies and snaps to corners, edge midpoints and edges, so a hinge pin lands exactly on a corner. With several bodies selected the pivot is only for that selection, and a new selection removes it. Grouping or bundling them keeps it: the group takes the pivot as its own, with X, Y and Z under “Posição”.

Laying flat on a face

Should a part lie on its best side for printing? Select it, click “Deitar sobre a face” and then the face that should go down. While you move the pointer over the part, the face under it lights up, so you see which one you are about to pick; turn the view to check the other sides. The part turns so that this face rests on the workplane.

Two parts should meet face to face, or line up flush? Select the part that should move, click “Alinhar faces”, then click its face and after that the face of the other part. In the panel, “Face com face” puts the faces against each other (back to back, touching), “Rente” lays them in one plane side by side; “Espaço” leaves room between them, for example for clearance. “Alinhar” moves the part: it turns the shortest way until the faces are parallel - not at all if they already are - and then slides only towards the other face, so it keeps its place sideways. The other part stays where it is, and one Undo takes it all back.

Holes on one axis: “Mesmo eixo” lines up round faces instead: a hole over a hole, a pin in a hole, a screw in its clearance hole. Choose it before you click the faces. Then click the round inside of a hole (or the outside of a pin) on the part that moves, and a round face of the other part; the whole round face lights up while you point at it. “Alinhar” turns the part the shortest way until the two axes are parallel and slides it straight across onto the other axis. Along the axis it stays where it was, so a pin keeps its height; push it in afterwards. This works on imported meshes too, as long as the hole is made of facets around one axis.

An exact angle between two faces: “Alinhar faces” leaves the faces parallel; there is no tool of its own for an angle between them, but this gets you there. First bring the faces together with “Alinhar faces”, so they are parallel. Then set the pivot on the edge where they meet (“Definir ponto de rotação”, click the edge or a corner) and type the angle, 15 or 30 say, in the “Rotação” card of the part. It turns about that pivot. The card's axes are the axes of the plate, so this works when the edge runs along X, Y or Z.

Just as simple are “Baixar até o plano de trabalho” (D), which drops the selection onto the workplane, and “Centralizar no plano de trabalho”. With the workplane on a face, it centres on the point you picked with W and moves the selection only along that face, so what stands on it stays on it.

C picks the selection up, like Cruise in Tinkercad: it follows the mouse pointer and lies down on the face of another body you are over, sloped ones too. A click sets it down there, Esc leaves it where it was. Its underside then rests on the face, lined up with the face's edges as a new shape would be; it keeps its own turn about its up axis. Several parts travel together and keep their spacing. Over the empty plate, C stands a tilted part upright on the workplane again. Without a keyboard, the menu of a long press has “Colocar sobre uma face” for the same.

Patterns: row and circle

For hole grids, bolt circles and rings of teeth there is the “Padrão”. Select the shapes to be multiplied and click it in the ribbon.

  • “Fileira”: The selection is repeated at equal distances. The row steps along all three axes at once, with “Espaçamento X”, “Espaçamento Y” and “Espaçamento Z (altura)”: one of them gives a straight row, two or three a diagonal row or a staircase. A negative spacing lays the row the other way.
  • “Círculo”: The selection is spread around a centre. How many copies there are you set under “Quantidade (com o original)”, and the “Ângulo total” is 360° for a full circle. Choose whether the copies turn along. “Subida por cópia” lifts every copy a little more than the one before (a screw or an ascending spiral), and “Mudança de raio por cópia” moves it further from the centre or closer to it (a flat spiral); together they give a conical spiral. A spiral winds once around at most: an angle above 360° counts as a full circle, and there are at most 100 copies.

Tip: first set the pivot on a face with “Definir ponto de rotação”, then the centre of the circle lies exactly there.

The copies first appear as a preview. Only “Criar” creates them. If the copies are holes, group them afterwards with the body they should cut into.

With “Agrupar cópias” ticked, “Criar” groups the original and all copies into one object in the same step, as “Grupo de união” would: a ring of 36 pieces is then one part you can move, colour or cut with. Above 5 pieces the box ticks itself; once you tick or untick it yourself, it stays as you set it. “Desagrupar” brings the pieces back. If grouping does not work out, the pattern still comes in as separate pieces and the message says why.

Hole pattern on a face

For vents, speaker grilles, baskets and enclosures the “Padrão de furos” spreads holes evenly over a flat face. You no longer have to make one hole, repeat it and set it onto the wall. Select exactly one body and click “Padrão de furos” in the “Modificar” group of the toolbar, next to the pattern. You can also right-click the body, or find it with Ctrl+K.

  1. Click a flat face of the body. The whole flat face around your click counts, not just the triangle under the pointer. A curved face does not work: you get a hint and click a flat one instead. On a group or a bundle a face runs on across parts that meet, as on one body; on a bundle only when no hole is among them. The parts are joined shortly after the tool opens; until then only one part's face lights up under the pointer, and a click waits for the join. On a group or bundle with text in it, only one part's face lights up, and the click joins the parts.
  2. In the window, set how the holes look and lie:
  • “Forma do furo”: “Redondo”, “Quadrado”, “Retângulo”, “Hexágono” or “Oblongo”.
  • Size: the “Diâmetro (Ø)” of a round hole, the “Comprimento do lado” of a square one, “Largura” and “Altura” for a rectangle and a slot. For the hexagon the size is the “Diâmetro entre cantos (Ø)”.
  • “Espaçamento centro a centro”: the distance from one hole's middle to the next. If it is smaller than the holes allow, PrintCAD raises it to the smallest possible value so a wall stays between the holes. For rectangles and slots it counts along the row, from left to right.
  • “Rows centre to centre”: rectangles and slots only: the distance from one row to the next. So narrow, tall holes stand close side by side while the rows keep apart. A hole 2 mm wide and 14 mm high needs 4 mm along the row and 16 mm from row to row with a 2 mm wall.
  • “Parede entre os furos”: the same distance said another way: the wall left between two neighbouring holes, which is the wall thickness in the print. Set it and the spacing centre to centre follows (for rectangles and slots the row spacing too), and the other way round. For hexagons, rectangles and slots PrintCAD measures the least distance between their edges.
  • “Disposição”: “Grade” puts the holes in rows and columns. “Intercalada” shifts every second row by half a spacing and moves the rows closer, so every hole has neighbours at the same distance - with hexagons that makes a honeycomb.
  • “Margem até a borda da face”: the clear distance between the holes and the edge of the face. Only holes that lie wholly inside the face minus this margin are made; anything that would reach beyond it is left out. The margin also holds around openings in the face.
  • “Profundidade”: “Atravessando o sólido inteiro” (the default) cuts through the whole wall. With “Profundidade personalizada” you give the depth in millimetres, for recesses, say.
  1. The holes show at once as a preview on the face and follow every value. The window says how many holes fit. “Outra face” chooses another face, Esc closes the tool, and like the other tool windows it moves by its title bar.
  2. Only “Criar” makes the holes.

The holes are ordinary holes grouped with the body, just as after “Grupo de união”: nothing is baked in. “Desagrupar” dissolves the group again and frees every hole, which you can then move, change or delete one by one. The whole thing is one step for “Desfazer”. The tool does not make more than 400 holes at once; make the holes bigger or the spacing wider then.

An AI can do the same through the MCP bridge with layerling_perforate_face (see the chapter AI with MCP).

Pushing a face out or in

For a thicker wall, a raised pad on a face or a pocket cut into it, the “Face” tool works on one flat face. It works on an imported STL too, where there is no sketch to change. Select exactly one body and click “Face” in the “Modificar” group of the toolbar, next to the hole pattern. You can also right-click the body, or find it with Ctrl+K.

  1. Click a flat face of the body. As with the hole pattern, the whole flat face around your click counts; a curved face does not work. On a group or a bundle the face runs on across parts that meet, as with the hole pattern.
  2. In the window:
  • “Direção”: choose “Para fora: adicionar material” for material standing on the face or “Para dentro: cortar uma cavidade” for a pocket cut into it. A pocket goes no deeper than the body behind the face; on a hollow body it can still reach the cavity.
  • “Recuo a partir da borda da face”: how far the new outline stays inside the face's edge. Going out, 0 takes the whole face, which makes a wall thicker. Openings in the face grow by the same amount.
  • “Raio dos cantos”: rounds every corner of the outline, the inner ones too, so a sharp tip ends in an arc. A notch narrower than twice the radius closes, and a part of the face narrower than twice the inset and the radius together is left out.
  • “Profundidade”: how far the new part stands out of the face, or how deep the pocket goes.
  • “Como um sólido próprio (não agrupado)”: with the switch on, the new part (or the hole of a pocket) is a body of its own beside the body instead of a group with it, so you can resize it, move it or use it to cut at once.
  1. The result shows at once as a preview and follows every value. “Outra face” chooses another face, Esc closes the tool.
  2. Only “Criar” makes it.

What the tool adds is an ordinary sketch body, grouped with the body unless “Como um sólido próprio (não agrupado)” is on (then “Editar grupo” has nothing to open, and the part is free): nothing is baked in. “Editar grupo” opens the group, and “Editar esboço” on the new part shows the outline it follows. On the top or the bottom of a plain sketch body - one area, not grouped, mirrored, tapered, twisted or leaning, and without a stroke, key ring, fillet or chamfer - that is the body's own outline, set in when it is built, so its lines and arcs stay exact wherever the CAD kernel can set them in, for the edge tools and the STEP export. Resized later, the part keeps its inset. On any other face, and on every face of an imported mesh, the outline follows the face's triangles, and a curve is made of short straight pieces. The whole thing is one step for “Desfazer”. To change the inset or the radius later, undo and run the tool again. To round the top edge afterwards, use “Arredondamento” with “Arestas de cima” on the group as the last step: like every edge treatment on a group, it joins the group into one body.

An AI can do the same through the MCP bridge with layerling_push_face (see the chapter AI with MCP).

Changing several parts at once

With several parts selected, the properties panel on the right shows how many there are and offers what applies to all of them together: “Sólido” or “Orifício”, one colour for all (a click on “Sólido” opens the colours) and the lock. With a mixed selection, one click makes them all solids or all holes. If any of the parts is locked, only the lock can be used. Sizes are changed with the handles, with “Redimensionar por porcentagem” or by typing a number into a size label. With several parts the number is the size of the whole selection: every part grows with it, the spaces between them too, as when you drag a handle. Solid and hole also work with a right-click or the S and H keys.

Scaling by percent

To make one or several parts larger or smaller by the same share, say all the parts of a print file by 20 %, use “Redimensionar por porcentagem”. Select the parts, right-click them and choose “Redimensionar por porcentagem”, or search for it with Ctrl+K.

Under “Novo tamanho” you type the new size as a percentage of the current one: 120 % is a fifth larger, 50 % half the size. Width, depth and height change alike, so the proportions stay. The bottom stays where it is, so nothing sinks into the plate.

With several parts you choose how they grow:

  • “Juntas”: The parts grow around their common centre, and the gaps grow with them. The whole becomes larger as if it were one part.
  • “Cada uma no lugar”: Every part grows where it stands, and the gaps between them stay the same.

The percentage field has the focus when the window opens, so from the keyboard it is Ctrl+K, the percentage, Enter; Esc closes the window. Only “Redimensionar” (or Enter in the field) changes the design, and one step back undoes it. With a locked part in the selection it does not open.

Undoing

“Desfazer” (Ctrl+Z) and “Refazer” (Ctrl+Shift+Z or Ctrl+Y) step through your history. How many steps travel with the saved design you set in the settings under “Histórico”.

Looking back: the history view

“Histórico”, next to undo and redo, opens a view of your history without changing anything. The work area shows the design as it was, and a slider at the bottom runs through every undo step: all the way to the right is the current state, each step to the left is one undo earlier, and states you have undone sit to the right of the current one. The arrow keys step through the states, Esc returns to the current state. The mouse wheel over the slider does the same, one state per notch, up to an earlier one; Page Up and Page Down jump ten states, Home goes to the oldest state and End back to the current one. You can turn and zoom the view meanwhile and click objects to bring them back (see below), but nothing can be moved; the design itself stays exactly as it is. If the bar covers something, drag it away by its title bar; a double-click on it brings it back.

Two things you can take along from an earlier state. “Novo projeto a partir deste estado” puts that state into a new design in the overview, named after this one with the time of that state in brackets - a design saved before PrintCAD recorded times says "copy" instead - and with the history up to that point, so undo keeps working there. “Exportar este estado” opens the usual export with that state as the design, so you can save it as STL, 3MF, STEP, a picture or a LYL file with the history up to that state.

You can also bring single objects back into the design. Click an object in the state shown - Shift+click adds more or takes one off again, a click on empty space picks nothing - and choose “Trazer para o projeto”. The view closes and a copy of each object lands in the current design exactly as it was in that state and at the spot it had there, selected, next to whatever the design holds now; nothing else changes. It is one step, so undo takes it back. Use it for a version of a part you changed since, or for one you deleted. A group comes back whole; notes and reference points on an object stay behind. Its name carries the time of that state, such as “Box (12:32)”, so it is easy to tell from the version the design has now. To bring back a part from inside a group, select the group and choose “Abrir grupo” or press E, as in the design: its parts are laid out where editing the group would put them, ready to pick, and the bar says which group you are in. “Sair” closes it again. This is only to look - the group, the design and the history stay as they are.

“Excluir histórico” sits alone at the bottom left of the bar. After a question it removes every earlier step for good: the design keeps the state it has now (not the one shown), Undo has nothing to go back to and a file saved afterwards carries no history. Use it to share a design without its past, or to start the undo steps over. It cannot be undone; to share a file without touching your own design, choose “Nenhum” when exporting instead.
