Placing and adjusting shapes

The shape library, dimensions by number and by handle, colour, rotating, tapering and twisting.

Every part in PrintCaddy starts with a shape. From them you build everything else: shapes complement each other, cut each other out or are joined into a new shape.

Adding a shape

Click “Şekil ekle” in the ribbon. The shape library opens.

The library closes after each shape you pick. Its title bar moves it, so you can put it where it does not cover the spot you are aiming at, over the Properties panel for example; a double-click on the title bar puts it back under its button, and where you left it is kept in the browser. The pin in the title bar keeps the library open while you put several shapes one after the other. Its edges and corners pull its size, as wide and as high as your screen allows; the size is kept in the browser too, and a double-click on an edge sets it back. Pulled narrow, the library leaves out the names and shows only the pictures; the name appears when you point at a shape.

Pick a shape. It now hangs on the mouse pointer and lands where you click. Move it over a body and it lies down on the face under the pointer, sloped ones too, like Cruise in Tinkercad; over the empty plate it lands on the workplane. Esc cancels placing. A tool that waits for a click, such as the tape measure, the framing square, a note, “Çalışma düzlemi yerleştir”, “Yüzey üstüne yatır” or the edge tools, is switched off when you pick a shape, so the click places the shape. The other way round, switching one of those tools on puts the shape down again. If you would rather have the shape appear in the middle of the plate, you can switch off placing by click in the settings (area “Görünüm”, switch “Yeni şekil eklerken yüzeylere yerleştir (Cruise)”).

On a face the shape snaps to the middle of that face when the pointer comes near it, as a corner or an edge snaps; hold Alt to set it down anywhere. Dragged from the library onto a face instead, it always lands in the middle of the face, or with Alt where you let go.

What the library offers:

  • Basic shapes: “Kutu”, “Yuvarlatılmış kutu”, “Silindir”, “Kapsül”, “Elips”, “Çokgen” (three to twenty-four sides), “Küre”, “Koni”, “Piramit” (with three to twenty-four sides, so three-sided too), “Kama”, “Yuvarlak çatı”, “Yarım küre”, “Paraboloid” (a dome that narrows to a rounded tip, its height falling with the square of the distance from the axis) and “Simit”.
  • Tubes: “Boru” and “Bükülmüş boru”, made of up to twelve straight pieces with bends in between, and the “Geçiş (loft)” from one outline to another, say from square to round.
  • Decorative shapes: “Yıldız”, “Kalp” and “Hilal”.
  • Lettering: “Metin”, also along a circular arc. More in Text.
  • Mechanics: “Diş” (threaded rod, screw, nut and tapped hole), “Yay”, “Gridfinity” bins and baseplates, “Dişli çark” and the “Tırtıl” for grips. More in Threads and mechanics.
  • For constructions: the print-in-place “Menteşe”, “Kırlangıç kuyruğu”, the “Damla delik” for horizontal holes, the “Silindirik havşa” and “Konik havşa” for screw heads and the “Cetvel”, which is only a measuring tool and never shows up in an export.

You get a triangle in two ways: the “Çokgen” with three sides is an isosceles triangle, the "Roof" of Tinkercad, and the “Kama” is a right triangle. Both are true triangular prisms. For a triangular hole, copy the shape, make the copy smaller, mark it as a hole and line it up with “Çalışma düzleminde ortala” or “Hizala”. Both work with the shape's bounding box, the smallest box that encloses it, so a halved square still counts as a whole square. The command search (Ctrl+K) also finds both shapes under "triangle" or "roof".

The “Kapsül” can be narrower at one end than at the other, say for a guard over a belt between two pulleys. Under “Özellikler” set “Küçük uç (çap)”; the large end is as wide as the capsule, and the sides run straight from one arc to the other. “Merkezler arası mesafe” is the distance between the two arcs' centres, that is how far apart the pulleys are. Change the small end and the centre distance stays, the capsule getting longer or shorter. The tapered capsule stays an exact body too, for fillets and STEP.

The shape's settings

As soon as a shape is selected, its settings appear on the right. At the top is the name; the pencil beside it (“Yeniden adlandır”) lets you type a new one. Enter keeps it, Esc cancels, and an empty name brings back the shape's default name. Further right, the padlock locks the shape against accidental moving and the eye hides it. The arrow on the far left folds the settings down to their title bar. The cards below fold open and shut with a click on their title (“Özellikler”, “Konum”, “Döndürme”, “Daralma”, “Burgu ve eğim” and the cards of the special shapes); PrintCaddy remembers which ones you left open, for the next shape and the next visit.

The settings sit docked at the right edge. If they cover something you want to see, drag them by their title bar and they float wherever you drop them; they stay there for the next shape too. Drop them back at the top right, or double-click the title bar, and they dock again. While they float or are folded down, the snap step moves from the bottom of the settings to the bottom right of the workplane.

  • “Katı” or “Delik”: A solid stays, a hole takes material away. A click on “Katı” opens the colours; colours of your own that you mix there wait under “Son kullanılanlar” afterwards, the last eight. More in Solids and holes. The pin in the row “Saklananlar” keeps the colour you have chosen for good: it stands in a row of its own, newer colours never push it out, and the same pin lets go of it again; up to 21 colours are kept in this browser.
  • “Saydam”: Lets you see through the body, for example to spot a shape behind it.
  • “Çok renkli”: For a group or a bundle; Shift+M switches it. Switched on, every part of the group shows in its own colour, also in a group that was cut with holes into one body (the walls a hole leaves take the colour of the body they were cut into). Picking a colour for the group switches it off and colours the whole group; the parts keep their own colours underneath, so switching it on again brings them back. A bundle has it switched on to begin with, and when it is switched off the bundle shows and exports in one colour. In a 3MF export every part keeps its colour, so the slicer can map the parts onto filaments; OBJ carries the colours on its points, so the border between two colours is a little rougher there.
  • “Özellikler”: The dimensions and everything that belongs to this shape. For a cylinder the diameter, the height and the number of sides. For a round tube or ring the “Dış çap”, the “İç çap” (for a bushing on a shaft of a known size) and the wall thickness: any two give the third, in whatever order you type them. The outer diameter keeps the bore and changes the wall, the inner diameter keeps the outer one and changes the wall, and a bore wider than the tube makes the outer diameter grow by the wall. The tube's length is its height. A tube that is not round, after you pulled one of its sides, has the sides Length and Width instead of the one outer diameter; there the wall stays when you change the width. For a gear the teeth, for a spring the turns. A value you have moved away from its default gets a small arrow next to its name: a click takes just that value back, whatever else you did since, unlike Undo. A double click on a slider does the same, here and for the sliders in the workspace settings. Dragged with the mouse, the slider of a length snaps to the snap step, so a height comes out in whole millimetres (not with the grid off, and not on short sliders); a typed value and the arrow keys on the slider stay exact. “Varsayılan olarak kaydet” at the bottom makes the current values the start values of this kind of shape (the same as “Şekil varsayılanları” in the settings), and “Varsayılanları sıfırla” goes back to the app's own. With “Delik gruplanınca boşluk gibi keser” the bore counts as a hole once the tube is grouped: it then also cuts the wall of what the tube is grouped with, so two tubes in a T-junction open into each other. A bent tube with an opening has the same switch.
  • “Elips yap”: Only on a cylinder. A cylinder is always round: it has one diameter, and every handle changes both sides together. For an oval, click this and it becomes an “Elips” - the same body, whose length and width you then set one by one. A cylinder with a chamfer, a fillet or a wall, or a turned or grouped one, cannot be changed this way; take the “Elips” from the start for those.
  • “Daralma”: Different sizes at the top and bottom, for example for a slope or a funnel.
  • “Burgu ve eğim”: Twists the top against the bottom or shifts it sideways. That gives twisted columns and leaning towers.

Taper and twist work on almost every shape. Only the gear, thread, spring, Gridfinity, hinge, pyramid, bent tube, teardrop, the screw-head cutters and the ruler leave them out: they have fixed measures of their own or, like the pyramid, a top of their own already.

“Varsayılan olarak kaydet” keeps what you changed on this shape as the start of every new shape of its kind: the sizes, the settings of the shape and its colour. Colour a cylinder green, press the button, and every cylinder you add from then on is green; the other kinds keep their own colours. “Varsayılanları sıfırla” takes the kind back to what the app starts it with. The kinds that have defaults of their own are marked in the settings under “Şekil varsayılanları”. A new design that you start from the start page begins with them too; the values are kept in this browser.

A tapered or leaning box, cylinder, ellipse, polygon, tube or ring keeps its exact shape, and so do the capsule, star, heart, crescent, dovetail a rounded box without rounded top and bottom edges, and a straight or round knurl (its chamfered ends narrow with it): chamfers and fillets work on it as on the plain shape, and the STEP export writes it. The same holds for a twisted shape: its section turns exactly as it rises, and the sides wind evenly from bottom to top.

Typing is more exact than dragging. All number fields take millimetres, but also percentages: type "50 %" into a width of 40 mm and you get 20 mm. They calculate too: "15*3", "120-2*4" or "(40+2)/2" give 45, 112 and 21; x works instead of *. The field remembers the calculation, in the Properties panel as well as in the dimension boxes around the selected body: click into it again later and the formula stands there instead of the number, as long as the value still matches it, and you change it in place, "(140+2)/2" becoming "(40+6)/2". Drag the body by a handle or scale it and the formula no longer fits, so the field shows just the number again. In a number field you have clicked, the mouse wheel and the keys ↑ and ↓ change the value by one step, with Shift a larger step and with Alt a finer one. In a size or position field Shift steps by the snap grid instead, and by ten steps when the grid is off.

Choosing colours

A click on “Katı” in the properties of a body opens the colour card, headed “Şekil rengi”. On top it shows the hex value of the colour the body has now. Under it are the ready colours of the palette; a click on one colours the body and closes the card.

“Özel” opens the colour picker of your browser, for a colour of your own. What it offers depends on the browser: in Chrome and Edge it has a colour field, a slider for the hue, an eyedropper that takes a colour from anywhere on the screen, and the value as HEX, RGB or HSL, which you switch with the arrows beside it. The colour counts when you close the picker or click outside it.

A colour of your own that you mix there waits under “Son kullanılanlar” afterwards, the last eight. The pin in the row “Saklananlar” keeps the colour you have chosen for good: it stands in a row of its own, newer colours never push it out, and the same pin lets go of it again; up to 21 colours are kept in this browser. The pin is for colours of your own, the ones of the palette are always there. With several colours selected, or with a hole, there is no single colour to keep.

The colours of the work area in the settings have a simpler picker of their own, with a colour field, a row of ready colours and a field for the hex value; see The settings.

How round is round? The side count

Round shapes such as cylinder, cone, tube, ellipse or the bores have the switch “Kenarlar boyutu takip eder” and the slider “Kenarlar” in their properties. The “Simit” has two sliders: “Halka kenarları” around the ring and “Boru adımları” around the tube. They follow the size there too until you hold them, so a large torus shows no facets. The reason: for the view and for STL, 3MF and OBJ, PrintCaddy draws a circle as a polygon of many small sides. The more sides, the smoother it is, and the more triangles it takes.

The “Boru kalınlığı” sets how thick the tube is seen from above. The height only squashes or stretches the tube, so a flat ring keeps its hole; at half the outer size the hole closes. Dragging the handles scales the tube along. Only a torus whose tube is as thick as the torus is high stays an exact body for chamfers, fillets and STEP.

Where the side count matters

  • In the export for the slicer (STL, 3MF, OBJ). A cylinder with few sides arrives there as a polygon.
  • When bodies and holes are combined. A bore cut by a hole with few sides is angular in the finished part. That includes screw holes.
  • In the view. Many very fine shapes slow the editor down.

Where it does not matter

  • For the STEP export. It writes the round shape, not the polygon.
  • For chamfers and fillets (“Pah kır”, “Yuvarlat”) as long as the shape stays round. That is the case when you have not set a side count, when the count is at least the shape's default, or when the polygon strays at most 0,05 mm from the circle. The default is 96 sides for cylinder, ellipse, tube and cone, 64 for the “Yuvarlak çatı”, 24 steps for the “Küre”, 32 for the “Yarım küre”, 96 sides and 24 steps for the “Simit”, and a “Kalite” of 32 for a round “Bükülmüş boru”.
  • For a thread, a spring or a helical gear. The edge tool and the STEP export take its exact body; its “Kalite” only sets how finely it is drawn.

Where it matters again

  • For chamfers and fillets on a shape with few sides. If you set the side count below the default, the edge tool takes the shape as it is drawn: a cylinder with 6 sides stays a hexagonal bar, and the fillet runs round its six edges. That is how you can build, for instance, a nut pocket with rounded corners. For a round shape, leave “Kenarlar boyutu takip eder” on.

How to use it

  • Leave “Kenarlar boyutu takip eder” on. Then PrintCaddy picks the side count from the diameter so that the polygon strays at most 0,005 mm from the true circle, far below what a printer resolves. Small shapes get at least 24 sides, large ones more.
  • Fewer sides you take when you want a polygon on purpose (but there is the “Çokgen” for that) or when the editor gets slow with very many round shapes.
  • More sides you hardly ever need. Only on very large round parts, when you see edges in the STL.
  • For parts you only treat with edges or hand on as STEP, the side count does not matter.

Working with the handles

Besides the settings there are handles on the shape itself:

  • The corners and edges make the shape larger or smaller. Drag one past the opposite side and the shape keeps growing there, on the other side of that edge. The arrow on top does the same below the base. Hold Shift while dragging a corner to scale width, depth and height together and keep the proportions; hold Alt to scale from the center instead of the opposite corner. Without a keyboard, or if you do not want to hold a key, switch on “Oranları koru” at the top of the properties: then the corners always keep the proportions, and typing one of width, depth or height scales the other two by the same factor. The setting is remembered.
  • The black arrow above the shape lifts or lowers it; with Alt held from the start, a copy is made where you let go, so you can stack. The white handle on the top face changes the height.
  • The curved arrows rotate it.
  • The numbers beside the shape show the dimensions. A click on one opens a field in which you type the number you want. With Enter the shape grows from the edge opposite the number, as when you drag; with Alt+Enter it grows round its middle, so a shape that was aligned with another stays aligned. With “Oranları koru” ticked in the properties, or with Shift+Enter, the other two dimensions follow by the same factor. That holds for width and length; the height always grows upward from the underside. The fields in the properties on the right keep the middle without a key. Tab keeps the number and opens the next one (width, depth, height); Shift+Tab goes back. In a field of the properties that you have clicked, the mouse wheel changes the value as well: a unit a notch, ten times as much with Shift (in a size or position field the snap grid), a tenth with Ctrl and a hundredth with Alt. A field that you have not clicked leaves the wheel to the panel, which scrolls.
  • A click on a corner (without dragging) opens width and depth together. Type the first, press Tab for the second and Enter to apply both. Esc cancels.

Once a shape is turned by right angles, for instance laid on its side with “Yüzey üstüne yatır”, the fields in the properties are named after the direction each measure runs in now: “Yükseklik” is what goes up, “Genişlik” runs along X and “Uzunluk” along Y. Typing the height of a shape lying down therefore changes the vertical measure, and its underside stays on the plate. A shape tilted at any other angle shows its own measures in the fields, and a note below says so.

Without the mouse, use the keyboard: the arrow keys move the selection by one snap step (0.01 mm when the grid is off), with Shift by five, or by ten if you set “Daha koyu ızgara çizgisi her” to “10 ızgara adımında” in the settings under “Görünüm”: as far as the next darker line, as in Tinkercad. They follow the view: → moves to where the screen's right is, ↑ away from you, even after turning the plate. Push a shape out of the picture and the view moves along. Ctrl+↑ and Ctrl+↓ raise and lower it. A run of arrow presses is one step in the history: as long as the next press follows within a second and a half, Undo takes back the whole run, not a press at a time. R rotates by 45°, Shift+R by 22.5°. D drops the selection onto the workplane.

The handles pull a shape as large as your plate, at least 220 mm wide and deep. Typing a number in the properties goes beyond that, and a shape made that large does not shrink back when you grab it again. A ceiling of your own per shape is set in the settings under “Şekil varsayılanları” with “Özel boyut sınırı”.

The snap step is at the bottom right of the editor and can be changed at any time.

Copying and duplicating

“Kopyala”, “Yapıştır” and “Çoğalt” are in the ribbon (“Pano”). Ctrl+D duplicates a selection. PrintCaddy remembers how you last moved or rotated the copy and applies the same again on the next duplicate. That produces a row of holes or steps in a few key presses. For regular arrangements there is also the pattern, described in Selecting and arranging.

Tip: If a shape refuses to take the size you want, it is often the limits for new shapes. Under “Şekil varsayılanları” in the settings you decide how each shape starts and how large it may get.

Custom shapes

A holder, a grip or a base plate you need again and again goes into the “Özel şekiller”. They sit at the top of the shape library, above the “Temel şekiller”, and fold away with their arrow.

  1. Select one or more bodies, holes, groups or an imported model included.
  2. Open the shape library and click “Seçimi kaydet”. Enter a name and confirm with “Kaydet”.
  3. In any design, a click on the tile inserts the shape, like any other shape. You can also drag the tile onto the workplane; it then lands where you let go. The shape then hangs on the pointer, like any other: it follows it, also over the faces of other bodies, and a click sets it down; Esc puts it away.

Several bodies come back together, placed as they stood to each other, and stay selectable one by one. Notes pinned to the saved bodies go along and come back closed, pinned to the inserted ones; free notes and reference points stay in the design. The pencil on a tile renames it, the bin removes it after asking. Shapes already inserted into designs stay as they are.

The “Özel şekiller” live in this browser, apart from the designs, and are there again the next time you start. “Tümünü yedekle” on the start page backs them up with the designs, and opening the backup brings both back. To take only the shapes to another browser, “Özel şekilleri yedekle” (the arrow pointing down) saves them as a ZIP file and “Şekilleri yükle” (the arrow pointing up) brings them back there. “Şekilleri yükle” also takes single .lyl designs; each one becomes a shape. Shapes already there are not added twice.

Folders

Many custom shapes sort better into folders. “Yeni klasör” (the folder with the plus, next to “Seçimi kaydet”) asks for a name and makes a folder at the top; the folder with the plus on a folder, “İçine yeni klasör”, makes one inside it. Folders go three levels deep: a folder on the third level holds shapes but no further folders. Folders sit above the shapes that are in none, in the order of their names, and open and close with their arrow; the number on a folder counts the shapes in it and in the folders inside it. When saving, choose the folder in the list next to the name; “Klasör yok” keeps the shape at the top.

The folder with the arrow on a tile moves that shape into another folder, or back out with “Klasör yok”. The pencil on a folder renames it, the bin deletes it after asking, together with the folders and shapes in it. A folder itself cannot be moved: make the new one where it belongs and move the shapes over. A backup keeps the folders, and loading it puts the shapes back in the same place.

Custom shapes on the server

With the shared server store switched on, the section shows two groups: “Sunucuda” and “Bu tarayıcıda”. When saving you choose where the shape goes; the server is chosen to start with. There it lives in the folder Custom shapes, as a .lyl file of its own with a picture, ready on every device that reaches the server; its folders are folders inside Custom shapes. Each side has folders of its own, and a shape moves between the folders of its side. The cloud on a browser tile puts that shape on the server; it is then no longer kept twice.

The Custom shapes folder shows on the start page as well. Open a shape there like a design, change it, and it is saved automatically like any design on the server; the next insert takes the new version. As the server store has no login, everyone who reaches the server sees the same shapes.

Wrapping around a cylinder

Should a pattern, a logo or lettering go onto a cup, a can or a tube? First lay it flat on the plate, as an imported SVG or as text for example, the way it should look from above. Then wrap it around a cylinder in the shape's settings under “Silindirin etrafına sar”:

  1. Open “Silindirin etrafına sar” and enter the “Çap” of the cylinder wall, such as the outside diameter of your cup. Tenths of a millimetre work too, such as 63.5.
  2. Click “Sar”. What ran from left to right now runs around the cylinder, the back edge becomes the top, and the thickness stands out of the wall.
  3. Select the wrapped shape and the cylinder and centre both, left to right and front to back. The middle of the wrapped shape is the cylinder's axis, so it then sits right on its wall.

For an engraving, switch on “İçe doğru (oyma)”: the thickness then goes into the wall. Make the shape a hole, centre it and group it with the cylinder.

A shape that is wrapped already is a mesh of the wrapped thing and cannot be wrapped again: undo the wrapping, or wrap the flat original once more with another diameter.

The result is a mesh like an imported model. Undo lays the shape flat again; for another diameter, undo first and wrap again. It cannot go further than once around the cylinder; then PrintCaddy names the smallest diameter that fits.

A surface texture

Grooves, knurling, honeycomb or bumps on a body, for a better grip or for looks: open “Surface texture” in the shape's settings. The button “Surface texture” in the “Değiştir” group at the top does the same: it opens and closes the card for the selected body and stays lit while the card is open.

  1. Choose the “Pattern”, its “Spacing” and its “Depth”. “Direction” says whether it goes into the body or stands out of it. For “Bumps”, “Bump shape” chooses between “Half ball”, “Cone (pointed)”, “Softly rounded” and “Flat on top”.
  2. Under “Laid on”, take “Around an axis” for round bodies such as a cup, a knob or a handle. The pattern runs round the “Axis”, and the spacing is rounded a little so that a whole number of grooves or cells goes round: the pattern closes without a seam. “All round” suits bodies that are not round, “Flat, along the axis” a lid or a plate seen from above. Leave it at “Automatic” and PrintCaddy chooses for you: “Around an axis” on round bodies and on angular ones standing upright such as a box - the pattern then follows the outline and runs round the edges without a seam, a pattern standing out bending round them -, “All round” on other angular ones.
  3. Click “Apply texture”. A large body takes a few seconds; you can keep working meanwhile.

To give the pattern to only some faces, say only the top of a box, click “Pick faces” and then the faces in the view: the top, the bottom or a side. A face reaches up to the body's sharp edges; on a cylinder the mantle is one face, and the top and the bottom are one each. Picked faces light up, a second click takes one out again, and “Every face again” gives the pattern the whole body again. Only the picked faces get the pattern; all others stay as they were and keep few triangles. On a top or a bottom the pattern lies flat; on a round top, grooves, rope and knitting run in rings round the middle, like a tree's year rings. “Direction on top/bottom (°)” turns the pattern on the top and bottom: at 0° the rows run along the front edge, at 90° along the side edge. “Top and bottom” switches grooves, rope and knitting there between rows and rings; on an angular body the rings follow its outline. “Edge at top and bottom” says what happens at the edge between the side and a patterned top or bottom: “Sharp” lets both end at the edge. With “Side runs over” the side's pattern bends round the edge and takes the whole top: rows of rope, knitting and level grooves run on as rings, upright and slanted grooves as rays almost to the middle. “Short fold-over” does the same as a rim only; inside lies the top's own pattern, as “Top and bottom” sets it. “Border” lays a border round the edge for every pattern – a cord, a cast-off chain or a round bead.

Under “Daha fazla” you choose where the pattern goes: outer and inner walls, how far the bottom and the top stay smooth (a smooth bottom keeps the body flat on the bed), a band of the height, and whether sharp edges stay smooth. Next to everything that stays smooth, the pattern fades in gently; “Fade in over” sets over how many millimetres. The smaller it is, the closer the pattern comes to an edge; a pattern standing out runs out right up to it. On the flat sides of an angular body like a cube it ends at a straight border that far away instead: grooves and diamonds stop with a short wall, and only whole bumps stay. “Profile” shapes the cross-section of a groove or joint: pointed and flat come out exactly, round in as many steps as “Detail” gives it.

Besides “Grooves”, “Knurling”, “Honeycomb” and “Bumps” there are more patterns. “Cells” are irregular cells like paving stones or dried clay; their joints are cut cleanly as the honeycomb's are, and another “Arrangement” lays them out anew. “Knitting”, “Rope” and “Basket weave” copy knitted stitches, a wound rope and a basket of stakes and weavers; they start raised and round, at the size of their models. “Grain” roughens the surface finely, as if blasted; the grain lies in space, without a seam or stretching. “Image” takes a grey picture as a height map: one of the ready ones such as wood, topography or terrazzo, or your own with “Load your own image…”. Light becomes the full depth; “Tile width” sets how wide a tile is, and under “Tiles” the tiles repeat, mirror or are staggered.

“Carbon (twill)” lays carbon fibre woven in twill onto the body: upright bands and crossing bands, over and under each other, one band on from band to band, so the familiar diagonal shows. “Weave” chooses “Twill 2/2”, the even weave of most carbon parts, or “Twill 2/1” and “Twill 3/1”, where the upright bands show long and the crossing ones short. “Band width” sets how wide the upright bands are; “Crossing band width” makes the crossing ones wider or narrower for an uneven weave (0: as wide). Round a body the band width is rounded a little so the weave closes without a seam. The single fibres are far too fine for a print: the bands with their crossings are what shows.

“Bricks” lay a brick wall onto the body, with joints sunk in like mortar. “Bond” chooses how the bricks are laid: “Running bond” as most walls are, “Stack bond”, or with short bricks between the long ones (“English bond”, “English cross bond”, “Flemish bond”). “Brick length” and “Course height” count from joint to joint and are rounded a little so that whole bricks fit: round a cup, from edge to edge of a box's side and from the bottom to the top; “Joint width” says how wide the joints are. Under “Daha fazla”, “Uneven bricks (%)” lets every brick lie a little deeper, as in an old wall.

The pattern “Cracks (kintsugi)” grows cracks across the surface as in kintsugi, with kinks, branches and a width that tapers towards the end. “Start” tells where they begin: at the top rim of a vase or a cup, at the bottom, from the rim to the middle of a plate, or anywhere; under “Kind” they become lightning. With “Gold filling as a part of its own” the notch is filled by a part of its own in gold, for a second filament; it goes along when you change or remove the texture. Under “Daha fazla” you set branches, zigzag and chipped edges, and another “Seed” throws the cracks anew.

“Bark” lays a tree's bark round a log: “Oak”, “Pine”, “Spruce”, “Birch” or “Beech”, each at its own size and depth. “Wood grain” makes the body a piece of wood: under “Wood” choose oak, ash, beech, pine or walnut, under “Cut” a log without its bark or a board, flat-sawn with its arches or quarter-sawn with straight lines. “Ring width” makes the grain fine or coarse. With “Annual rings on the ends” the ends of a log show the annual rings, and they match the grain on its sides: the rings run through the whole body as in a real piece of wood. Under “Daha fazla” you add “Knots”, and the rings bend round them.

With the pattern “Text (stuck on)” you stick a text onto the body like a label pressed on: the surface is unrolled round the place, so the letters keep their size on a vase's belly, on a slanted wall or over the edge of a box. "Around" (0° is the front, 90° the right) and "Height" in % along the axis say where it goes; with the axis on X or Y, 0° is the top - that puts the text on a flat plate. “Direction” engraves it or raises it. Over a real corner it tears like a sticker; the status line says so then. “Harfler yukarıdan aşağıya” stacks the letters top to bottom here too.

To keep part of the body smooth, or to texture only a part, use a helper body: put a box, a cylinder or a sphere where it should be, select it and choose under “Texture mask” either “Keeps the texture away” or “Texture only here”. It turns transparent, and it counts when the other body gets its texture, also while it is hidden. Hide or delete it before printing.

The result is a mesh like an imported model, and it keeps the body it was made on. Select it later, also after saving, and the card shows the settings it was made with: change them and click “Change texture”, and the texture is made again on that body, where and as large as the textured body stands now. “Remove texture” gives the plain body back. Mask bodies count again when the texture is changed: hide them rather than delete them if you may change it later. Undo takes back each of these steps as well.

A vase

The “Vase” is a body of its own for vases, pots and lampshades. Its measures are under “Özellikler”, its form and pattern on the card “Form and pattern”; all of it can be changed at any time, also after saving:

  • “Body form”: bottle, bellied, bowl with neck, barrel, cylinder or funnel. The height and the widest diameter are the shape's own measures: drag the handles, or type “Diameter” and “Yükseklik”.
  • “Cross-section”: a circle, a polygon with rounded corners or a wave (soft, pointed, arches or fins). “Twist (°)” turns it up the height, “Sway (°)” lets it swing back and forth.
  • “Build”: “Solid (vase mode)” is for the slicer's vase mode (also called spiralize): the slicer prints one wall in a spiral and leaves out the top and the infill, so the body is drawn solid. “Hollow with wall” gives a real wall and bottom for a normal print.
  • “Pattern”: a new vase is plain. To choose from are grooves, stripes, Voronoi cells or facets (low poly) on the outside, in a band of the height if you like; they belong to the vase and stay editable with it.

“Cross-section on top” blends the cross-section into a second one up the height, say from a circle at the bottom to a hexagon at the top.

“Draw the vase”, high up in the vase's settings, opens a large window of its own over the work area: in it you draw the vase yourself, in real millimetres. You move it by its title bar and make it larger from its corner; the vase in the view follows every change. “Body form” shows it from the side: drag the points, the top one sets the height, the widest one the diameter; “Handle” gives a point an angle, “Corner” a kink. “Cross-section” shows the cross-section from above, and the “Symmetry” mirrors or repeats what you draw. “Twist” draws the twist as a curve up the height. An SVG file from a drawing program becomes the cross-section or the body form, the mesh of a model vase (STL, 3MF, OBJ) the body form. A photo of a vase can go behind the drawing to trace, or “Take outline” takes its outline. One drag of the mouse is one step of undo; while you drag, the view shows the vase roughly.

The card “Bottom and wall” holds what the body needs besides its form. On a hollow vase: drain holes with a slope towards them, a hollow foot, an open wall (“Open wall”: Voronoi, honeycomb, diamonds or circles, for lanterns and baskets) and a “Lampshade”, hanging, standing on a socket or over an LED puck. On a solid vase, “Stronger vase mode” cuts fine slits through which the slicer prints ribs inside or a double wall in its vase mode, in one go. Recesses for rubber feet go with either. “Must hold water” raises the wall and bottom of a hollow vase to at least 1.6 mm.

On the card “Accessories” the vase gets its accessories. “Legs” can be moulded on, so they are part of the vase, or made to plug in, with sockets in the bottom; a stand holds the vase from below. A “Saucer” catches the water, or hides in the foot. A hollow vase can take an “Insert”: a plant insert, one with a water store for a pot that waters itself, a holder for stems or water tubes. A “Lid” slips over the rim, snaps into a groove or screws onto a thread in the wall. “Lay out accessories” lays the parts out beside the vase as objects of their own, ready to print, upside down where that prints better. The vase already has the sockets, groove or thread. After changing a setting, lay them out again: the parts from before are replaced. If a part does not fit this vase, the editor says so, for example when an insert would not get through a narrow neck.

The card “Print” tells what the vase takes to print: its weight in the chosen “Material” and the length of filament – for a solid vase worked out for the slicer's vase mode, one line along the wall. Below are notes on printing it: an overhang of more than 45° and where it is, a wall that is too thin, what a lid, a lamp or legs need, and the slicer settings for ribs and a double wall.

“Detail” sets how finely the vase is divided into triangles: finer looks smoother, but makes the editor slower with large vases.
